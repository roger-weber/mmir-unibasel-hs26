Summary

Key Takeaways¶

  1. Transformers come in three families that differ in their attention mask: encoders see the whole input and suit classification and embeddings, decoders see only the past and generate text, and encoder-decoders transform one sequence into another, as in translation and speech recognition.

  2. Encoders are pretrained with masked language modeling, decoders with causal language modeling, encoder-decoders with span corruption or denoising, and embedding models with contrastive objectives. Distillation produces small models from large ones.

  3. Today’s transformers share pre-normalization, rotary position embeddings, gated feed-forward layers, and grouped-query attention, and large models increasingly use mixtures of experts.

  4. Fine-tuning adds a small head, for sequence classification, token classification, or span extraction, and continues training with small learning rates. A frozen encoder is cheap and shareable; full fine-tuning is more accurate; LoRA trains a fraction of a percent of the parameters with similar results.

  5. On the NLTK tweets, Naive Bayes reaches 99.85% because the labels are the emoticons by which the tweets were collected. Without emoticons, Naive Bayes and a frozen encoder reach 79%, a fine-tuned encoder 83%, zero-shot prototypes 68%, and a prompt to Claude Sonnet 79% (Haiku 75%) without any labeled example; on 17% of the tweets, both language models disagree with the label, because the sentiment was in the removed emoticon. The most important check comes before any classifier: how were the labels made?

  6. Among methods of similar accuracy, choose the simplest (Occam’s razor). Naive Bayes trains in milliseconds and classifies a tweet in microseconds, about 300,000 times faster than a prompt to a large model, and the four points of the fine-tuned encoder may not survive other data.

  7. A generative model produces text by repeatedly sampling the next token. Temperature and top-p sampling shape the distribution; greedy decoding is deterministic. Fluent but false continuations, hallucinations, follow from the mechanism.

  8. Instruction tuning and preference alignment turn a language model into an assistant, which expects its input in a chat template. The KV cache stores the keys and values of past tokens and dominates the memory for long contexts.

  9. Prompts classify and extract without training, can follow new instructions immediately, and can be forced into valid JSON by constrained decoding, but they are slow and costly per request, and the confidences they write are not probabilities.

Further Reading¶

Model Families and Pretraining¶

  • Vaswani, A., et al. (2017). Attention is all you need. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 30. arXiv. The transformer with its encoder, decoder, and cross-attention.

  • Devlin, J., Chang, M.-W., Lee, K., & Toutanova, K. (2019). BERT: Pre-training of deep bidirectional transformers for language understanding. Proceedings of NAACL-HLT. arXiv. Masked language modeling, the 80/10/10 masking rule, and the fine-tuning recommendations used in D.2.

  • Liu, Y., et al. (2019). RoBERTa: A robustly optimized BERT pretraining approach. arXiv. Shows that BERT was undertrained and improves it without changing the architecture.

  • Clark, K., Luong, M.-T., Le, Q. V., & Manning, C. D. (2020). ELECTRA: Pre-training text encoders as discriminators rather than generators. International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR). arXiv. Replaced token detection.

  • Sanh, V., Debut, L., Chaumond, J., & Wolf, T. (2019). DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT: Smaller, faster, cheaper and lighter. arXiv. Knowledge distillation: 40% smaller, 60% faster, 97% of BERT’s language understanding.

  • He, P., Liu, X., Gao, J., & Chen, W. (2021). DeBERTa: Decoding-enhanced BERT with disentangled attention. International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR). arXiv. Separate content and position vectors in attention.

  • Warner, B., et al. (2024). Smarter, better, faster, longer: A modern bidirectional encoder for fast, memory efficient, and long context finetuning and inference. arXiv. ModernBERT: an encoder with the architecture changes of decoders, 8,192 tokens of context, trained on 2 trillion tokens.

  • Raffel, C., et al. (2020). Exploring the limits of transfer learning with a unified text-to-text transformer. Journal of Machine Learning Research, 21. arXiv. T5 and span corruption.

  • Lewis, M., et al. (2020). BART: Denoising sequence-to-sequence pre-training for natural language generation, translation, and comprehension. Proceedings of ACL. arXiv. Denoising pretraining for an encoder-decoder.

  • Brown, T. B., et al. (2020). Language models are few-shot learners. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 33. arXiv. GPT-3 with 175 billion parameters and in-context learning.

  • Grattafiori, A., et al. (2024). The Llama 3 herd of models. arXiv. Open models up to 405 billion parameters trained on 15.6 trillion tokens.

  • Yang, A., et al. (2025). Qwen3 technical report. arXiv. Dense and mixture-of-experts models from 0.6 to 235 billion parameters.

  • Su, J., et al. (2021). RoFormer: Enhanced transformer with rotary position embedding. arXiv. Rotary position embeddings.

  • Xiong, R., et al. (2020). On layer normalization in the transformer architecture. Proceedings of the 37th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML). arXiv. Why pre-normalization trains more stably.

  • Shazeer, N. (2020). GLU variants improve transformer. arXiv. SwiGLU and other gated feed-forward layers.

  • Ainslie, J., et al. (2023). GQA: Training generalized multi-query transformer models from multi-head checkpoints. Proceedings of EMNLP. arXiv. Grouped-query attention.

  • Dao, T., Fu, D. Y., Ermon, S., Rudra, A., & Ré, C. (2022). FlashAttention: Fast and memory-efficient exact attention with IO-awareness. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 35. arXiv. Exact attention in tiles that fit into on-chip memory.

  • Jiang, A. Q., et al. (2024). Mixtral of experts. arXiv. A mixture-of-experts model with 47 billion parameters, of which each token uses 13 billion.

Fine-Tuning¶

  • Rajpurkar, P., Zhang, J., Lopyrev, K., & Liang, P. (2016). SQuAD: 100,000+ questions for machine comprehension of text. Proceedings of EMNLP. arXiv. The dataset behind extractive question answering.

  • Tjong Kim Sang, E. F., & De Meulder, F. (2003). Introduction to the CoNLL-2003 shared task: Language-independent named entity recognition. Proceedings of CoNLL. arXiv. The standard dataset for named entity recognition with BIO tags.

  • Houlsby, N., et al. (2019). Parameter-efficient transfer learning for NLP. Proceedings of the 36th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML). arXiv. Adapter layers.

  • Hu, E. J., et al. (2022). LoRA: Low-rank adaptation of large language models. International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR). arXiv. Low-rank updates with 10,000 times fewer trainable parameters than full fine-tuning of GPT-3.

  • Dettmers, T., Pagnoni, A., Holtzman, A., & Zettlemoyer, L. (2023). QLoRA: Efficient finetuning of quantized LLMs. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 36. arXiv. LoRA on a 4-bit base model, fine-tuning a 65-billion-parameter model on one 48 GB graphics processor.

  • Reimers, N., & Gurevych, I. (2019). Sentence-BERT: Sentence embeddings using siamese BERT-networks. Proceedings of EMNLP. arXiv. Fine-tuning encoders into bi-encoders for semantic search.

Text Classification¶

  • Pang, B., & Lee, L. (2008). Opinion mining and sentiment analysis. Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval, 2(1-2), 1-135. PDF. A comprehensive survey of sentiment analysis and its difficulties.

  • Go, A., Bhayani, R., & Huang, L. (2009). Twitter sentiment classification using distant supervision. Stanford University technical report. PDF. Emoticons as noisy labels for tweets, with the advice to remove them from the training input.

  • Bird, S., Klein, E., & Loper, E. (2009). Natural language processing with Python. O’Reilly. Free online edition. The NLTK book, with Naive Bayes classifiers and the corpora used in D.3.

  • Kim, Y. (2014). Convolutional neural networks for sentence classification. Proceedings of EMNLP. arXiv. TextCNN.

  • Wang, W., et al. (2020). MiniLM: Deep self-attention distillation for task-agnostic compression of pre-trained transformers. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 33. arXiv. The distilled encoder behind the MiniLM classifiers of D.3.

  • Barbieri, F., Camacho-Collados, J., Espinosa Anke, L., & Neves, L. (2020). TweetEval: Unified benchmark and comparative evaluation for tweet classification. Findings of EMNLP. arXiv. The benchmark and the RoBERTa sentiment model used in D.3.

Generative Language Models¶

  • Holtzman, A., Buys, J., Du, L., Forbes, M., & Choi, Y. (2020). The curious case of neural text degeneration. International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR). arXiv. Nucleus sampling and why greedy decoding degenerates.

  • Ouyang, L., et al. (2022). Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 35. arXiv. Instruction tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback.

  • Rafailov, R., et al. (2023). Direct preference optimization: Your language model is secretly a reward model. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 36. arXiv. Preference alignment without a separate reward model.

  • Wei, J., et al. (2022). Chain-of-thought prompting elicits reasoning in large language models. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 35. arXiv. Intermediate reasoning steps in the prompt.

  • Liu, N. F., et al. (2024). Lost in the middle: How language models use long contexts. Transactions of the ACL, 12. arXiv. Models use the middle of a long context worst.

  • Kwon, W., et al. (2023). Efficient memory management for large language model serving with PagedAttention. Proceedings of the 29th ACM Symposium on Operating Systems Principles (SOSP). arXiv. The KV cache in blocks, the basis of vLLM.

  • Willard, B. T., & Louf, R. (2023). Efficient guided generation for large language models. arXiv. Constrained decoding with regular expressions and grammars for structured output.

  • Allal, L. B., et al. (2025). SmolLM2: When smol goes big: Data-centric training of a small language model. arXiv. The small open model used in the examples of D.1 and D.4.

  • Radford, A., et al. (2023). Robust speech recognition via large-scale weak supervision. Proceedings of the 40th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML). arXiv. Whisper, an encoder-decoder trained on 680,000 hours of audio.
