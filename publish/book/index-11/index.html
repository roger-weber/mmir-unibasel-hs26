A - Machine Learning Fundamentals

Many chapters of this book use a learned model somewhere: a classifier routes queries, an encoder turns text into embeddings, a network describes an image. This chapter collects the vocabulary these models share. It answers two questions: what does it mean for a program to learn, and how do we know that what it learned holds for data it has not seen? The methods themselves follow in the next chapters of this appendix.

Mitchell gave the definition that frames this chapter in a 1997 textbook:

A computer program is said to learn from experience EEE with respect to some class of tasks TTT and performance measure PPP, if its performance at tasks in TTT, as measured by PPP, improves with experience EEE.

The task is what we want the system to do, from a simple classification to driving a car. The performance measure says how well the system does the task; it guides training and lets us compare two systems. The experience is the data the system sees during training and what it is told about that data: examples with correct answers, rewards, or no feedback at all.

Where this is used

  • The classifiers of Intent Routing and Classification, Learned Features: CNNs, and Classifying Audio learn from labeled examples; Evaluating Text Classifiers and Scores, Thresholds, and ROC Curves evaluate them once they make hard decisions.

  • The softmax and the cross-entropy loss train the word embeddings of Word Embeddings, and with a temperature they form the contrastive loss of Multimodal Transformers.

  • Self-supervised learning is how the models of Transformer Embeddings and Automated Feature Extraction learn without manual labels.

  • When Evaluation Misleads shows what happens when information from the test set leaks into training.

  • C - Neural Networks builds on the gradient of the cross-entropy loss.

Prerequisites

Derivatives and the chain rule, vectors and norms, and basic probability. No other appendix chapter is needed; the following chapters on methods and neural networks build on this one.

Tasks and Experience describes what a system can learn to do and what it learns from. Losses and Performance Measures defines the measures that training minimizes and that compare systems, from the mean squared error to the softmax and the cross-entropy loss. Over- and Underfitting turns to the central difficulty, that a model can perform well on its training data and still fail on new data. Training and Model Selection describes the training protocol that detects and avoids this.
