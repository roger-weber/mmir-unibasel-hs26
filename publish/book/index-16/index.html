G - Mathematical Toolbox

The chapters use a small set of mathematical tools again and again. Latent Semantic Indexing truncates a singular value decomposition and calls the result the best rank-kkk approximation. Approximate Nearest-Neighbor Search shrinks vectors with principal component analysis. From Sound to Spectrogram and Extended Image Features move signals and images into Fourier space and back. Decision trees, classifiers, and language models measure uncertainty with entropy and train with the cross-entropy. The chapters apply these tools and state their results. This chapter explains why the tools work and what exactly they optimize, without repeating how the chapters apply them.

The details matter when a tool is used outside its assumptions: when PCA runs on features with different units, when a convolution in Fourier space wraps around the border, when a spectrum shows frequencies that are not in the signal, or when a loss compares two distributions in the wrong direction.

Where this is used

  • Latent Semantic Indexing, Word Embeddings, and Approximate Nearest-Neighbor Search use the SVD and PCA; Eigenvectors, SVD, and PCA shows what they optimize and why the truncated SVD is the best low-rank approximation.

  • From Sound to Spectrogram, Perceptual Features, , and Extended Image Features use the Fourier transform, convolutions, windows, and the discrete cosine transform; Fourier Transform and Convolution explains why they work.

  • Decision Trees and Gradient Boosting, Naive Bayes, Cross-Entropy, Phrases and Compounds, and Generative Language Models use entropy, cross-entropy, and mutual information; Information Theory defines them and shows how they relate.

Prerequisites

  • Vectors, matrices, matrix products, and the dot product.

  • Complex numbers in the form eiφ=cos⁡φ+isin⁡φe^{i\varphi} = \cos\varphi + i \sin\varphieiφ=cosφ+isinφ, for the second section.

  • Probabilities and expected values, for the third section.

Eigenvectors, SVD, and PCA starts with the eigenvectors of symmetric matrices and the one inequality behind both the SVD and PCA, then shows why the truncated SVD minimizes the reconstruction error and why maximal variance and minimal reconstruction error are the same goal. Fourier Transform and Convolution treats the discrete Fourier transform as a change of basis, explains why the FFT is fast and why convolutions become products, and shows where leakage comes from and why the cosine transform of MFCC and JPEG approximates PCA. Information Theory defines entropy, cross-entropy, the Kullback-Leibler divergence, and mutual information, and shows why the cross-entropy is the standard loss.
