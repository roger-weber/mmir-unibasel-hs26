Eigenvectors, SVD, and PCA

Where this is used

Latent Semantic Indexing truncates the singular value decomposition of a term-document matrix and states that the result is the best rank-kkk approximation under the Frobenius norm; this section shows why, and what the statement leaves out. Word Embeddings projects embeddings onto two principal components for a plot, and Approximate Nearest-Neighbor Search shrinks vectors with principal component analysis before the search. Extended Image Features diagonalizes the matrix of the quadratic distance with its eigenvectors. Losses and Performance Measures measures dimensionality reduction by the reconstruction error, which PCA minimizes. Fourier Transform and Convolution shows that the cosine transform of MFCC and JPEG approximates PCA.

The SVD and PCA answer one question: which few directions describe a set of vectors best? Both answers rest on a single property of symmetric matrices. We first derive this property, then show what the SVD optimizes and why the truncated SVD is optimal, and finally show that PCA’s two goals, maximal variance and minimal reconstruction error, are the same goal.

Eigenvectors of Symmetric Matrices¶

A vector u≠0\mathbf{u} \neq \mathbf{0}u=0 is an eigenvector of a square matrix C\mathbf{C}C if the matrix only stretches it, Cu=λu\mathbf{C}\mathbf{u} = \lambda \mathbf{u}Cu=λu, with the eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ as the stretch factor. For a symmetric matrix, C=C⊤\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{C}^\topC=C⊤, the spectral theorem guarantees real eigenvalues λ1≥λ2≥⋯≥λd\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_dλ1​≥λ2​≥⋯≥λd​ and an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors u1,…,ud\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_du1​,…,ud​. Collected as the columns of an orthogonal matrix U\mathbf{U}U:

C=UΛU⊤=∑i=1dλiuiui⊤\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{U} \boldsymbol{\Lambda} \mathbf{U}^\top = \sum_{i=1}^{d} \lambda_i \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{u}_i^\topC=UΛU⊤=i=1∑d​λi​ui​ui⊤​
(1)

Applied to a vector, the decomposition reads from right to left: rotate the vector into the eigenvector axes, stretch each axis by its eigenvalue, and rotate back. The matrices that the chapters decompose are of this kind: covariance matrices, the co-occurrence matrices AA⊤\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^\topAA⊤ and A⊤A\mathbf{A}^\top\mathbf{A}A⊤A of a term-document matrix, and the similarity matrix of the quadratic distance. The first two are also positive semidefinite, x⊤Cx≥0\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{C} \mathbf{x} \ge 0x⊤Cx≥0 for every x\mathbf{x}x, so their eigenvalues are nonnegative.

The property behind both the SVD and PCA concerns the quadratic form u⊤Cu\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{C} \mathbf{u}u⊤Cu of a unit vector u\mathbf{u}u. We write u\mathbf{u}u in the eigenvector basis, u=∑iciui\mathbf{u} = \sum_i c_i \mathbf{u}_iu=∑i​ci​ui​ with ∑ici2=1\sum_i c_i^2 = 1∑i​ci2​=1. Because the eigenvectors are orthonormal, the quadratic form becomes

u⊤Cu=∑i=1dλici2\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{C} \mathbf{u} = \sum_{i=1}^{d} \lambda_i c_i^2u⊤Cu=i=1∑d​λi​ci2​
(2)

This is a weighted average of the eigenvalues with the weights ci2c_i^2ci2​. A weighted average cannot exceed its largest term, so the maximum is λ1\lambda_1λ1​, reached when all weight lies on u1\mathbf{u}_1u1​.

Key Formula: Rayleigh Quotient and Its Maximum
max⁡∥u∥=1u⊤Cu=λ1,max⁡Q⊤Q=Ik∑j=1kqj⊤Cqj=λ1+⋯+λk\max_{\lVert \mathbf{u} \rVert = 1} \mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{C} \mathbf{u} = \lambda_1, \qquad \max_{\mathbf{Q}^\top \mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{I}_k} \sum_{j=1}^{k} \mathbf{q}_j^\top \mathbf{C} \mathbf{q}_j = \lambda_1 + \dots + \lambda_k∥u∥=1max​u⊤Cu=λ1​,Q⊤Q=Ik​max​j=1∑k​qj⊤​Cqj​=λ1​+⋯+λk​
(3)

For a symmetric C\mathbf{C}C, the first maximum is reached by the top eigenvector u1\mathbf{u}_1u1​. The second, over kkk orthonormal directions q1,…,qk\mathbf{q}_1, \dots, \mathbf{q}_kq1​,…,qk​, is reached by the top kkk eigenvectors (Ky Fan’s theorem).

The second statement follows from the same argument. Each eigenvector ui\mathbf{u}_iui​ receives a total weight of at most 1 from the kkk directions, and the total weight over all eigenvectors is kkk. The sum is therefore largest when the weight sits on the kkk largest eigenvalues, one unit each. The top kkk eigenvectors are the best kkk directions, and they are nested: the best direction is part of the best pair, which is part of the best triple.

The Singular Value Decomposition¶

A term-document matrix A\mathbf{A}A of size m×nm \times nm×n is neither square nor symmetric, but two symmetric matrices come with it. A⊤A\mathbf{A}^\top\mathbf{A}A⊤A of size n×nn \times nn×n holds the dot products between documents, and AA⊤\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^\topAA⊤ of size m×mm \times mm×m holds the dot products between terms, a weighted count of how often two terms occur in the same documents. Both are positive semidefinite.

Let vi\mathbf{v}_ivi​ be the orthonormal eigenvectors of A⊤A\mathbf{A}^\top\mathbf{A}A⊤A with the eigenvalues σi2\sigma_i^2σi2​, and define ui=Avi/σi\mathbf{u}_i = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}_i / \sigma_iui​=Avi​/σi​ for every σi>0\sigma_i > 0σi​>0. These vectors are orthonormal as well:

(4)

Rewriting Avi=σiui\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}_i = \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_iAvi​=σi​ui​ for all iii at once gives AV=US\mathbf{A}\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{S}AV=US, and since V\mathbf{V}V is orthogonal, the singular value decomposition A=USV⊤\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{V}^\topA=USV⊤ of Latent Semantic Indexing. The SVD exists for every matrix. The right singular vectors vi\mathbf{v}_ivi​ are the eigenvectors of A⊤A\mathbf{A}^\top\mathbf{A}A⊤A, the left singular vectors ui\mathbf{u}_iui​ are the eigenvectors of AA⊤\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^\topAA⊤, and the singular values are the square roots of their common eigenvalues. The latent topics of LSI are therefore the eigenvectors of the term co-occurrence matrix: terms that occur in the same documents load on the same directions, which is why LSI places them close together.

Written as a sum, the SVD decomposes A\mathbf{A}A into rank-1 layers of decreasing weight:

A=∑i=1rσiuivi⊤,∥A∥F2=∑j,lajl2=∑i=1rσi2\mathbf{A} = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^\top, \qquad \lVert \mathbf{A} \rVert_F^2 = \sum_{j,l} a_{jl}^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2A=i=1∑r​σi​ui​vi⊤​,∥A∥F2​=j,l∑​ajl2​=i=1∑r​σi2​

The layers are orthogonal to each other, and each layer uivi⊤\mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^\topui​vi⊤​ has a Frobenius norm of 1, so the squared entries of A\mathbf{A}A add up to the sum of the squared singular values. The singular values say how much of the “energy” of the matrix each layer carries.

What the Truncated SVD Optimizes¶

Latent Semantic Indexing keeps the first kkk layers, Ak=UkSkVk⊤\mathbf{A}_k = \mathbf{U}_k \mathbf{S}_k \mathbf{V}_k^\topAk​=Uk​Sk​Vk⊤​. The Eckart-Young theorem states that no matrix of rank kkk comes closer to A\mathbf{A}A.

Key Formula: Eckart-Young Theorem
min⁡rank⁡(B)≤k∥A−B∥F2=∥A−Ak∥F2=∑i>kσi2\min_{\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{B}) \le k} \lVert \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B} \rVert_F^2 = \lVert \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{A}_k \rVert_F^2 = \sum_{i > k} \sigma_i^2rank(B)≤kmin​∥A−B∥F2​=∥A−Ak​∥F2​=i>k∑​σi2​
(6)

The truncated SVD minimizes the sum of the squared differences over all entries. The error is the energy of the discarded layers.

The proof needs two steps. First, a matrix B\mathbf{B}B of rank kkk has its columns in a kkk-dimensional subspace. For a fixed subspace with an orthonormal basis Q\mathbf{Q}Q of kkk columns, the closest B\mathbf{B}B projects every column of A\mathbf{A}A onto the subspace, B=QQ⊤A\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^\top\mathbf{A}B=QQ⊤A, since the orthogonal projection is the closest point of a subspace. Second, the projection and the residual are orthogonal, so by Pythagoras

∥A−QQ⊤A∥F2=∥A∥F2−∥Q⊤A∥F2=∥A∥F2−∑j=1kqj⊤AA⊤qj\lVert \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^\top\mathbf{A} \rVert_F^2 = \lVert \mathbf{A} \rVert_F^2 - \lVert \mathbf{Q}^\top \mathbf{A} \rVert_F^2 = \lVert \mathbf{A} \rVert_F^2 - \sum_{j=1}^{k} \mathbf{q}_j^\top \mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^\top \mathbf{q}_j∥A−QQ⊤A∥F2​=∥A∥F2​−∥Q⊤A∥F2​=∥A∥F2​−j=1∑k​qj⊤​AA⊤qj​
(7)

∥A∥F2\lVert \mathbf{A} \rVert_F^2∥A∥F2​ is fixed, so minimizing the error means maximizing the energy that the subspace captures. By the Rayleigh argument applied to AA⊤\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^\topAA⊤, this energy is at most σ12+⋯+σk2\sigma_1^2 + \dots + \sigma_k^2σ12​+⋯+σk2​, reached by the top kkk left singular vectors. The error is then ∑i>kσi2\sum_{i > k} \sigma_i^2∑i>k​σi2​. The same matrix Ak\mathbf{A}_kAk​ is also optimal in the spectral norm, the largest stretch of A−B\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}A−B, with the error σk+1\sigma_{k+1}σk+1​.

Example: A small term-document matrix

Five terms (car, auto, engine, flower, garden) and four documents: d1d_1d1​ contains car and engine, d2d_2d2​ auto and engine, d3d_3d3​ car, auto, engine, and flower, and d4d_4d4​ flower and garden, each once. The matrix has ∥A∥F2=10\lVert \mathbf{A} \rVert_F^2 = 10∥A∥F2​=10 and the singular values 2.550, 1.461, 1.000, and 0.600, whose squares add up to 10. The rank-1 approximation keeps 2.5502/10=652.550^2 / 10 = 652.5502/10=65 percent of the energy and has the error 1.4612+12+0.62=1.870\sqrt{1.461^2 + 1^2 + 0.6^2} = 1.8701.4612+12+0.62​=1.870; the rank-2 approximation keeps 86 percent and has the error 1.166.

Other rank-1 approximations do worse. Projecting all documents onto the direction of a single document vector, the best choice d3d_3d3​ gives the error 1.937, the others 2.345 and more. In the rank-2 space, the rows of U2S2\mathbf{U}_2 \mathbf{S}_2U2​S2​ for car and auto become identical (a cosine of 1.000, against 0.500 in A\mathbf{A}A), because d1d_1d1​ and d2d_2d2​ differ only in car versus auto, and the two leading dimensions do not distinguish them.

The theorem also says what the truncated SVD does not optimize. It minimizes the squared error over all entries, each entry weighted equally. Large entries dominate the error, so frequent terms and long documents shape the leading dimensions unless TF-IDF weighting and normalization tame them first. The objective knows nothing about queries or relevance: LSI finds the directions that reconstruct the matrix best, and the improvement in retrieval is a consequence that experiments confirm, not a guarantee.

The folding-in formula of Latent Semantic Indexing follows from the decomposition. Multiplying A=USV⊤\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{V}^\topA=USV⊤ from the left with Uk⊤\mathbf{U}_k^\topUk⊤​ and Sk−1\mathbf{S}_k^{-1}Sk−1​ gives Vk⊤=Sk−1Uk⊤A\mathbf{V}_k^\top = \mathbf{S}_k^{-1} \mathbf{U}_k^\top \mathbf{A}Vk⊤​=Sk−1​Uk⊤​A, so every document d\mathbf{d}d of the collection maps to its row of Vk\mathbf{V}_kVk​ by d⊤UkSk−1\mathbf{d}^\top \mathbf{U}_k \mathbf{S}_k^{-1}d⊤Uk​Sk−1​. A new document or a query receives the same map. It is placed where it would lie if it had been part of the collection, provided that it does not change the directions Uk\mathbf{U}_kUk​.

Principal Component Analysis¶

PCA asks the same question for a cloud of nnn points in ddd dimensions. We collect the points as the rows of a matrix and subtract their mean, so that the rows xi\mathbf{x}_ixi​ of X\mathbf{X}X are centered. The covariance matrix C=X⊤X/(n−1)\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X} / (n - 1)C=X⊤X/(n−1) is symmetric and positive semidefinite. The variance of the points along a unit direction u\mathbf{u}u is the mean squared length of their projections:

1n−1∑i=1n(u⊤xi)2=u⊤Cu\frac{1}{n - 1} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{x}_i)^2 = \mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{C} \mathbf{u}n−11​i=1∑n​(u⊤xi​)2=u⊤Cu
(8)

By the Rayleigh argument, the direction of maximal variance is the top eigenvector of C\mathbf{C}C, the first principal component, and its variance is λ1\lambda_1λ1​. The next components are the next eigenvectors, each orthogonal to the previous ones and each of maximal remaining variance.

The second view of PCA asks for the direction whose projections reconstruct the points best. Each point splits into its projection onto u\mathbf{u}u and a residual perpendicular to it, and by Pythagoras

∥xi∥2=(u⊤xi)2+∥xi−(u⊤xi) u∥2\lVert \mathbf{x}_i \rVert^2 = (\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{x}_i)^2 + \lVert \mathbf{x}_i - (\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{x}_i)\, \mathbf{u} \rVert^2∥xi​∥2=(u⊤xi​)2+∥xi​−(u⊤xi​)u∥2
(9)

Summed over all points, the left side is the total squared distance from the mean, which does not depend on u\mathbf{u}u.

Key Formula: Variance and Reconstruction Error
∑i∥xi∥2⏟total, fixed=∑i(u⊤xi)2⏟captured variance × (n−1)+∑i∥xi−(u⊤xi) u∥2⏟reconstruction error\underbrace{\sum_{i} \lVert \mathbf{x}_i \rVert^2}_{\text{total, fixed}} = \underbrace{\sum_{i} (\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{x}_i)^2}_{\text{captured variance} \,\times\, (n - 1)} + \underbrace{\sum_{i} \lVert \mathbf{x}_i - (\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{x}_i)\, \mathbf{u} \rVert^2}_{\text{reconstruction error}}total, fixedi∑​∥xi​∥2​​=captured variance×(n−1)i∑​(u⊤xi​)2​​+reconstruction errori∑​∥xi​−(u⊤xi​)u∥2​​
(10)

With a fixed total, maximizing the captured variance and minimizing the reconstruction error are the same goal. For kkk components, the error is (n−1)(λk+1+⋯+λd)(n - 1)(\lambda_{k+1} + \dots + \lambda_d)(n−1)(λk+1​+⋯+λd​).

PCA and the SVD are the same computation. The SVD of the centered data matrix, X=USV⊤\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{V}^\topX=USV⊤, gives the principal components as the columns of V\mathbf{V}V, the variances as λi=σi2/(n−1)\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2 / (n - 1)λi​=σi2​/(n−1), and the coordinates of the points in the new axes as the rows of US\mathbf{U}\mathbf{S}US. Numerical libraries compute PCA this way, because forming X⊤X\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}X⊤X explicitly squares the ratio between the largest and the smallest singular values and loses precision in the small ones. LSI differs from PCA in one step: it does not center the term-document matrix. Its first dimension therefore points roughly toward the average document and reflects how many and which frequent terms a document contains, rather than a contrast between topics.

What PCA Assumes¶

The derivation makes the assumptions of PCA visible.

  • Squared distances in common units. The variance adds up squared coordinates, so a feature measured in larger units dominates. Multiplying the yyy coordinates of the example by 10, as a change of units would, turns the first component from 40.3°40.3°40.3° to 85.7°85.7°85.7°, almost the yyy axis. Features with different units are standardized to unit variance first; the bins of a color histogram or the dimensions of an embedding share their units and are used as they are.

  • Variance is what matters. PCA is unsupervised. The directions of largest variance need not separate the classes of a classification task; a direction with little variance can carry the decisive difference.

  • Linear structure. PCA finds flat subspaces. Data on a curved surface needs more components than its intrinsic dimension.

  • No outliers. A single distant point contributes its squared distance and can turn a component toward itself.

For retrieval, PCA has a further property. Projecting onto kkk components can only shorten a difference vector, so the distance in the reduced space is a lower bound of the distance in the original space. A search can use the cheap reduced distances to discard candidates that cannot be among the nearest neighbors and compute the exact distance only for the rest, the filter-and-refine principle of Extended Image Features. Approximate Nearest-Neighbor Search quantifies what the reduction saves.

Computing the SVD in Practice¶

A full SVD of an m×nm \times nm×n matrix costs on the order of m⋅n⋅min⁡(m,n)m \cdot n \cdot \min(m, n)m⋅n⋅min(m,n) operations, which Word Embeddings names as a limit of LSI. Retrieval needs only the top kkk layers of a large, sparse matrix. Iterative methods such as Lanczos iteration and randomized SVD (Halko et al., 2011) compute them from repeated products of A\mathbf{A}A and A⊤\mathbf{A}^\topA⊤ with a few vectors. The matrix stays sparse, and the cost grows with the number of its nonzero entries times kkk instead of with m⋅n⋅min⁡(m,n)m \cdot n \cdot \min(m, n)m⋅n⋅min(m,n); scikit-learn’s TruncatedSVD takes this route. Dense embeddings with a few hundred dimensions allow the direct route: the PCA stage of FAISS computes the d×dd \times dd×d covariance matrix of a training sample and its eigenvectors, and applies the projection to all vectors.
