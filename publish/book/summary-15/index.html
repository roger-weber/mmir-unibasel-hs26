Key Takeaways¶

Eigenvectors, SVD, and PCA¶

  1. A symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors, C=UΛU⊤\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{U}^\topC=UΛU⊤. Its quadratic form u⊤Cu\mathbf{u}^\top\mathbf{C}\mathbf{u}u⊤Cu is a weighted average of the eigenvalues, so the top kkk eigenvectors are the kkk orthonormal directions with the largest sum.

  2. Every matrix has an SVD A=USV⊤\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{V}^\topA=USV⊤. The singular vectors are the eigenvectors of AA⊤\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^\topAA⊤ and A⊤A\mathbf{A}^\top\mathbf{A}A⊤A, and the squared singular values add up to the squared entries of A\mathbf{A}A. LSI’s topics are the eigenvectors of the term co-occurrence matrix.

  3. The truncated SVD is the best rank-kkk approximation in the Frobenius norm (Eckart-Young), with the error ∑i>kσi2\sum_{i>k}\sigma_i^2∑i>k​σi2​. It minimizes the squared error over all entries, weighted equally; it knows nothing about queries or relevance.

  4. PCA finds the directions of maximal variance of centered data. By Pythagoras, captured variance plus reconstruction error is constant, so maximal variance and minimal reconstruction error are the same goal.

  5. PCA is the SVD of the centered data matrix, with the variances σi2/(n−1)\sigma_i^2 / (n-1)σi2​/(n−1). LSI is the SVD without centering.

  6. PCA assumes common units, treats variance as importance, finds only linear structure, and is sensitive to outliers. Distances after the projection are lower bounds of the original distances.

Fourier Transform and Convolution¶

  1. The DFT is a change to an orthogonal basis of complex waves: it loses nothing, and Parseval’s theorem preserves the energy.

  2. The FFT splits a transform of length NNN into two of length N/2N/2N/2 and needs N2log⁡2N\frac{N}{2}\log_2 N2N​log2​N instead of N2N^2N2 multiplications, 205 times fewer for N=1024N = 1024N=1024.

  3. Complex waves are the eigenvectors of every convolution, so a convolution becomes a pointwise product in Fourier space. The product computes a circular convolution; zero padding to L+K−1L + K - 1L+K−1 avoids the wrap-around.

  4. Autocorrelations are inverse transforms of the power spectrum and cost O(Nlog⁡N)O(N \log N)O(NlogN) for all lags.

  5. Cutting a frame multiplies the signal with a window, which convolves the spectrum with the window’s spectrum. The rectangle leaks through side lobes at -13.3 dB; Hann and Hamming windows lower them to -31.5 and -42.4 dB with a central peak twice as wide.

  6. The DCT uses real cosines of the mirrored signal. For strongly correlated neighbors it nearly equals the PCA basis, which is why MFCC and JPEG can truncate it.

Information Theory¶

  1. The entropy H(X)=−∑plog⁡2pH(X) = -\sum p \log_2 pH(X)=−∑plog2​p is the expected information of an observation and the smallest average code length per symbol.

  2. The cross-entropy H(p,q)H(p, q)H(p,q) is the average code length when data from ppp are coded for qqq. It exceeds the entropy by the KL divergence, which is never negative and not symmetric.

  3. Minimizing the cross-entropy, maximizing the likelihood, and minimizing DKL(p^ ∥ q)D_{\text{KL}}(\hat{p} \,\|\, q)DKL​(p^​∥q) are the same goal; this is why the cross-entropy is the standard loss. With soft targets, the minimum is the entropy of the target.

  4. The perplexity 2H2^H2H is the number of equally likely choices with the same uncertainty.

  5. The mutual information I(X;Y)=H(Y)−H(Y∣X)I(X;Y) = H(Y) - H(Y ∣ X)I(X;Y)=H(Y)−H(Y∣X) is the information gain of decision trees, the expected value of the PMI, and a criterion for feature selection; contrastive losses maximize a lower bound on it.

Further Reading¶

Eigenvectors, SVD, and PCA¶

  • Strang, G. (2023). Introduction to linear algebra (6th ed.). Wellesley-Cambridge Press. Book page. Eigenvectors, the spectral theorem, and the SVD, with the MIT lectures online.

  • Strang, G. Matrix methods in data analysis, signal processing, and machine learning (MIT 18.065). MIT OpenCourseWare. Video lectures on the SVD, PCA, and the Eckart-Young theorem.

  • Eckart, C., & Young, G. (1936). The approximation of one matrix by another of lower rank. Psychometrika, 1(3), 211-218. The theorem behind the truncated SVD.

  • Shlens, J. (2014). A tutorial on principal component analysis. arXiv. PCA derived from the covariance matrix and from the SVD, with its assumptions.

  • Halko, N., Martinsson, P.-G., & Tropp, J. A. (2011). Finding structure with randomness: Probabilistic algorithms for constructing approximate matrix decompositions. SIAM Review, 53(2), 217-288. arXiv. Randomized SVD for large matrices.

Fourier Transform and Convolution¶

  • Smith, J. O. Mathematics of the discrete Fourier transform (DFT), with audio applications (2nd ed.). Online book. The DFT as a change of basis, the convolution theorem, and their proofs.

  • Smith, J. O. Spectral audio signal processing. Online book. Windows, leakage, and the short-time Fourier transform.

  • Cooley, J. W., & Tukey, J. W. (1965). An algorithm for the machine calculation of complex Fourier series. Mathematics of Computation, 19(90), 297-301. The fast Fourier transform.

  • Harris, F. J. (1978). On the use of windows for harmonic analysis with the discrete Fourier transform. Proceedings of the IEEE, 66(1), 51-83. The classic comparison of window functions.

  • Ahmed, N., Natarajan, T., & Rao, K. R. (1974). Discrete cosine transform. IEEE Transactions on Computers, C-23(1), 90-93. The DCT and its closeness to the optimal (PCA) basis.

Information Theory¶

  • Shannon, C. E. (1948). A mathematical theory of communication. Bell System Technical Journal, 27, 379-423 and 623-656. PDF. Entropy, coding, and channel capacity.

  • MacKay, D. J. C. (2003). Information theory, inference, and learning algorithms. Cambridge University Press. Free online edition. Information theory together with its use in machine learning.

  • Cover, T. M., & Thomas, J. A. (2006). Elements of information theory (2nd ed.). Wiley. The standard textbook, with entropy, KL divergence, and mutual information.

  • van den Oord, A., Li, Y., & Vinyals, O. (2018). Representation learning with contrastive predictive coding. arXiv. The InfoNCE loss and its bound on the mutual information.
