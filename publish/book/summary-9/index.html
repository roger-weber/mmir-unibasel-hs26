Summary

Key Takeaways¶

  1. A program learns when its performance PPP at tasks TTT improves with experience EEE (Mitchell). Tasks include classification, regression, clustering, density estimation, anomaly detection, generation, transcription, and dimensionality reduction.

  2. The loss used for training and the measure used to compare systems can differ. Regression trains with the mean squared error. Classification trains with the cross-entropy of soft assignments, because accuracy does not change smoothly with the parameters.

  3. The softmax turns logits into probabilities. With a one-hot target, the cross-entropy loss is the negative log probability of the correct class, and its gradient with respect to the logits is y^−y\hat{\mathbf{y}} - \mathbf{y}y^​−y.

  4. Experience ranges from full supervision through semi-supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning to self-supervised learning, which takes the targets from the data itself and trains the embedding models of this book.

  5. Underfitting means high bias, overfitting means high variance. The expected squared error is the squared bias plus the variance plus the noise, and the best capacity minimizes the sum. In the polynomial example with ten points, this is degree 3.

  6. The test set is used once, at the end. Hyperparameters are chosen on a validation set or by k-fold cross-validation, which estimates generalization more reliably but does not prevent overfitting.

Further Reading¶

  • Mitchell, T. M. (1997). Machine learning. McGraw-Hill. Book page. The source of the definition of learning by task, performance measure, and experience that frames this chapter.

  • Goodfellow, I., Bengio, Y., & Courville, A. (2016). Deep learning. MIT Press. Chapter 5, “Machine Learning Basics”. The catalogue of tasks that A.1 follows, together with capacity, overfitting, and cross-validation; the whole book is free online.

  • Zhang, A., Lipton, Z. C., Li, M., & Smola, A. J. (2023). Dive into deep learning. Cambridge University Press. Section “Softmax Regression”. Derives the softmax and the cross-entropy loss with runnable code; the book is free online.

  • Hastie, T., Tibshirani, R., & Friedman, J. (2009). The elements of statistical learning: Data mining, inference, and prediction (2nd ed.). Springer. Book page with free PDF. Chapter 7 treats the bias-variance decomposition and cross-validation in depth.

  • Kohavi, R. (1995). A study of cross-validation and bootstrap for accuracy estimation and model selection. Proceedings of the 14th International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence (IJCAI). PDF. A large experiment that compares numbers of folds and recommends stratified ten-fold cross-validation for model selection.

  • Belkin, M., Hsu, D., Ma, S., & Mandal, S. (2019). Reconciling modern machine-learning practice and the classical bias-variance trade-off. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 116(32), 15849-15854. arXiv. Shows the double descent of the test error beyond the point where a model fits its training data exactly.

  • Goodfellow, I., Pouget-Abadie, J., Mirza, M., Xu, B., Warde-Farley, D., Ozair, S., Courville, A., & Bengio, Y. (2014). Generative adversarial nets. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 27. arXiv. Introduces the generator and the discriminator that train each other.

  • Ouyang, L., et al. (2022). Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 35. arXiv. Describes reinforcement learning from human feedback, with a reward model learned from human rankings of answers.

  • Sutton, R. S., & Barto, A. G. (2018). Reinforcement learning: An introduction (2nd ed.). MIT Press. Free online edition. The standard textbook on agents, rewards, and the balance of exploration and exploitation.

  • Gupta, S. C. (2022). MLOps: Machine learning life cycle. ML4Devs. Article. Explains how the data, model, development, and operations cycles combine into one MLOps lifecycle.
